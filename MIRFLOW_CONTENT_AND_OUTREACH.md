# Mirflow Content & Outreach Direction

## The position

Mirflow helps finance-heavy and operations-driven companies decide what to build, train their teams to use AI well, and install systems that create measurable improvement.

The scarce skill is no longer finding a tool or wiring together a demo. It is deciding where AI belongs, making it safe enough to use, and helping the team build lasting capability.

## Message pillars

1. **Decide before you build.** Start with the workflow, bottleneck, data, and risk—not the newest model.
2. **Adoption is part of the system.** A workflow nobody trusts or knows how to improve is not finished.
3. **Governance creates speed.** Clear ownership, review paths, and boundaries make useful AI easier to deploy.
4. **Leave capability behind.** Clients should be more capable after the engagement, not more dependent on a vendor.
5. **Say no clearly.** Some work should stay human, stay manual, or wait for better data.

## Voice

Write like an experienced operator speaking to a CFO, controller, COO, or owner: intelligent, curious, optimistic, direct, calm about technology, and specific about the work.

Avoid AI hype, guaranteed ROI, “revolutionize,” “set it and forget it,” generic agent language, fake benchmarks, and pressure-heavy sales copy.

## Outreach rule

Outreach should earn a conversation by showing a useful observation about the recipient's work. It should not lead with a service menu or imply that every company needs automation.

1. Name a workflow or decision the person likely owns.
2. Offer one specific observation or question.
3. Make Mirflow's relevance clear in one sentence.
4. Ask for a small next step, with permission to ignore it.

## Ready-to-publish LinkedIn posts

### The wrong first question

The first AI question most companies ask is:

“What can we automate?”

The better question is:

“Where is the business paying a human to repeat a decision that could be made clearer?”

Good AI work starts with the workflow, the data it touches, the judgment that must stay human, and the person who owns the result.

The tool comes later.

### Adoption is a deliverable

An AI workflow is not finished when the demo works.

It is finished when the team knows when to use it, when not to trust it, what happens when it is uncertain, and who owns the next improvement.

Training and governance are not “change management” added at the end. They are part of the product.

### A useful no

Sometimes the right AI recommendation is:

“Not yet.”

If the process is unstable, the data is inaccessible, or nobody owns the outcome, automation will only make the mess move faster.

The best implementation plan includes the work that has to become clear before a system should be built.

## Permission-based outreach templates

### CFO / controller

**Subject:** A question about [process]

Hi [Name] — I noticed [specific observation about close, reporting, document review, or client operations].

The question I would be asking is not “where can we add AI?” but “which part of this process is repetitive enough to improve without moving judgment or control to a black box?”

Mirflow helps finance and operations teams answer that question, then build only what has a clear owner and review path.

Would it be useful to compare notes for 20 minutes? If this is not a priority, no problem.

### COO / operator

**Subject:** One workflow worth looking at

Hi [Name] — [specific workflow] caught my attention because it appears to involve [repetition, handoffs, or manual review].

Before recommending a tool, I would map the current process and identify where the team is spending time versus judgment.

That is the work Mirflow does: decide what is worth building, make adoption practical, and leave the team with a system they can own.

Open to a short conversation about whether [workflow] is worth examining?

### Follow-up

Hi [Name] — closing the loop here. My only useful question is whether [workflow] is a real bottleneck or just an interesting idea from the AI wave.

If it is a bottleneck, I am happy to share the short diagnostic we use to decide whether to build, buy, or leave it alone.

## Default CTA

“Bring us the workflow that is slow, repetitive, or risky. We will help you decide what to build, what to buy, and what to leave alone.”
